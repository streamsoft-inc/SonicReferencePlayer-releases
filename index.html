    0.1.58 2026-11-23

    • Sonic Reference Player Pro. Requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Intel or Apple silicon.
    Full notes

    Sonic Reference Player Pro. Requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Intel or Apple silicon.

    New

    • Send DTS-HD MA Whole (Pass-through Test), in the Help menu, off until ticked and remembered. Pass-through normally sends a DTS-HD Master Audio stream as its 48 kHz 5.1 core, so an Auro-3D master inside DTS-HD MA reached the receiver as plain DTS 5.1. Ticked, the whole stream is sent (7.1, up to 192 kHz, Auro-3D inside). This has not yet been seen working on a receiver: please try it. A stream already passing through restarts in the new form from where it was.

    • Skip goes to the next or previous track of a file that has tracks (an MTFF, a DDP, an MKV with chapters). Back within a second of a track's start goes to the one before. Any other file keeps the 5-second step.

    Changed

    • Switching between Binaural and speakers keeps playing. Only the monitoring changes. An Auro-3D file is read again for its new decode and carries on from where it was, after a short gap.

    • Ctrl-click Peak Hold clears the held numbers and they stay clear while stopped. The meters' fall after Stop was being held as a new peak (-66 and the like).

    Fixed

    • After pass-through the audio device could stay closed ("the HDMI interface is no longer active" in Audio I/O), most likely when the format or rate changed between files. The Player now tries again for up to five seconds, and says so if the device still will not open.

    What we would like to hear from testers

    • Send DTS-HD MA Whole: your receiver, what its display shows (format, channels, rate, Auro), and whether it plays at all. If it does not, the log (Help > Log File...) has the "Pass-through:" lines.
    • After pass-through: whether Audio I/O Settings shows the HDMI device again.

    0.1.57 2026-11-23

    • Sonic Reference Player Pro. Requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Intel or Apple silicon.
    Full notes

    Sonic Reference Player Pro. Requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Intel or Apple silicon.

    New

    • Energy and Phase in the object view. Two new tick boxes along the bottom of the room, both off until ticked. Energy shows where the finished mix's sound is: a patch at each speaker for the level it carries, one patch between two speakers for sound they share, coloured by band (orange-red low, amber mid, pale blue high), a white ring where a transient lands, and a cross at the centre of the energy. Phase joins speakers that carry the same signal: a pale line in phase, a red line out of phase in the low, mid or high band, and the worst pair named at the top left. A red line means those two speakers cancel in that band when folded down. Both measure the mix as delivered, before Solo and Mute. A menu beside them shows all bands or the low, mid or high band alone; the boxes and the band are remembered. Phase compares speakers at the same instant, so a spaced-microphone recording reads as unrelated.
    • Object size and divergence are heard. An ADM object with a width, depth or height is spread over the speakers around it by ITU-R BS.2127's rule, and divergence splits it into a centre and two flanking positions. An object with no size plays exactly as before. It follows the published rule, not Dolby's renderer.
    • The picture shows in the album column, where the cover is. The arrow under it moves the picture to the video window; closing that window brings it back. The Video button turns the picture off for the file showing; the next file with a picture shows it again.

    • Assign Speakers is a 3-D room. Seen from behind the listener, with the preset in use named above it (Current Setup for the layout as it stands), unassigned speakers hollow, and a Plan button for the view from above. Drag a speaker onto another to swap their outputs.

    Changed

    • Dragging the bottom edge of the window changes its height only. A side or a corner still scales the window.
    • The Monitor meters sit 40 px to the right of the Source meters.

    • Faster drawing on macOS 26. The app now draws through JUCE's own image; macOS no longer scans each repaint.

    Fixed

    • Removing an MKV with DTS or TrueHD audio could hang the Player. Two ffmpeg helpers started at the same moment each kept the other's pipe open, so closing the pipe never reached the helper and the app waited for it for ever. The pipes are now closed on exec and a helper's writer can no longer block past a stop. Reproduced in a test and fixed there; not yet checked in the app.

    • A hang when the Source layout changed with Energy or Phase showing (Ctrl-click Source). The room drew a reading taken for the old layout against the new one.

    Known issues

    • The video in an MKV does not always show. Still being investigated. If you see it, please send the file's name and the log (Help > Log File...), which now has "Video window:" lines.

    What we would like to hear from testers

    • Energy and Phase: whether the picture matches what you hear; a red line on material you know to be in phase (name the pair and the band shown); whether playback of a wide file breaks up with a layer ticked.
    • Assign Speakers: your device, the order you set, and any layout that plays to the wrong speaker with Monitor on Follow File.

    0.1.49 2026-11-21

    • Sonic Reference Player Pro. Requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Intel or Apple silicon. This build adds pass-through of Dolby streams to a receiver over HDMI, and answers a tester's report on ADM, MKV and AC Connect.
    Full notes

    Sonic Reference Player Pro. Requires macOS 11 (Big Sur) or later, on Intel or Apple silicon. This build adds pass-through of Dolby streams to a receiver over HDMI, and answers a tester's report on ADM, MKV and AC Connect.

    Pass-through to a receiver (new, for testing)

    A Dolby Digital, Dolby Digital Plus or Dolby TrueHD stream can be sent to a receiver over HDMI undecoded, so the receiver decodes it, Atmos included.

    • The PASS button appears above DIM when all of these hold: the selected file (or the MKV stream you chose) is one of those Dolby formats, the output device is an HDMI receiver that accepts it, and FFmpeg is installed as for DTS and TrueHD playback (see the User Guide).
    • Turn PASS on and press Play. The receiver's display should show the format.
    • While it plays, nothing of the Player's is in the path: no meters, no Desk, no volume, no Solo or Mute, no Binaural. Set the level on the receiver.
    • Seeking starts the stream again from the new place; the receiver may take a moment to lock.
    • Files on disk only, not files streamed from Artist Connection or the phone.
    • Moving from one file to the next is not gapless.

    • Turn PASS off to play the file through the Player as before.

    Tested so far on one receiver with one Dolby TrueHD with Atmos stream. Dolby Digital and Digital Plus have not been tried on a receiver yet. It relies on formats macOS offers on HDMI but does not document, so it may behave differently on other Macs and receivers.

    ADM

    • A 9.1.4 or 9.1.6 mix delivered as a bed plus fixed objects now plays as 9.1.4 or 9.1.6. When every object is fixed in place and a pair sits at the wide positions, Source: Auto chooses 9.1.4, or 9.1.6 with a top-middle pair as well. The wide objects play from the wide speakers alone. Choosing the layout by hand (Ctrl-click Source) still works.

    MKV

    • Auro-3D inside DTS-HD MA decodes with the surrounds and rears the right way round.

    AC Connect

    • The phone is told when the Player quits, so it stops trying.

    Window

    • The white bar beside the meters can be dragged further left, giving the meters more room. It stops 40 px from the album cover. Double-click it to reset.

    Known issue

    • A click between two ADM files that should join seamlessly (reported with 2L-179 tracks 1 and 2). The files themselves join cleanly, so the fault is ours; it is being investigated.

    What we would like to hear from testers

    • Pass-through: which Mac, which receiver, which format, and what the receiver's display showed. If PASS does not appear, or there is no sound, please send the log (Help > Log File...).
    • Does your 9.1.4 or 9.1.6 ADM now open with the wides on their own speakers?

    Sonic Reference Player Pro. This build adds choosing an MKV's audio stream and its picture, decodes Auro albums played from Artist Connection, and makes Play wait for the audio device after a sample rate change.

    MKV files

    • An MKV with several audio streams lists one row per stream under the file, above its chapters. Click a row to play that stream. A stream that cannot play is greyed, with the reason. The choice is kept with the list and the next session, and Measure and Render use it too.
    • Details names the stream playing: its title, else its format.
    • The picture opens in the video window. The MKV's video is copied, not re-encoded, into a cached file the first time (H.264 and HEVC only), then opens in step with the sound.
    • Cover art and liner notes: a cover attached to the MKV shows as the file's artwork; a PDF attached to it opens from the row's right-click menu, "Liner Notes...".
    • An Auro-3D stream inside an MKV is now checked again when you choose it. Before, a file first opened on another stream (a TrueHD stream, say) stayed "not Auro" whichever stream you then chose, and an Auro stream played its eight channels undecoded.

    • Dolby TrueHD plays its 7.1 bed. The Atmos objects are not decoded.

    Artist Connection and AC Connect

    • The decode happens at the reader, so the channel list, the waveforms, the meters and playback all see the real thing.

    • On headphones, an Auro master is rendered by Auro's own engine, not by ours, so it is spatialised once and by the renderer that made it.

    Channel Based, and not inventing names

    • A file that does not say what its channels are is listed as Ch 1..N. Eight channels could be 7.1 or 5.1.2 and the file does not say, so the Player does not guess. Speaker names appear where the file actually supports the claim — an ADM's beds and objects, or an Auro carrier stating its layout.

    • "Channel Based" in the monitor menu means exactly that: no layout claimed, no fold, each channel straight out to its own output.

    Your room

    • Click a row's Out column to send it to a different output. Useful when a rig is not wired in layout order, and the only way to patch an unlabelled multichannel master.
    • Speaker Layout… does the same thing for a whole layout at once.
    • Both are remembered, because they describe the room rather than the file.

    • Audio I/O… picks the device, rate and buffer size.

    Binaural

    • Auro renders it, by default. For headphone monitoring the Player uses Auro's own renderer — the same engine that decodes Auro masters, and the path Auro's encoder itself offers for 7.1.4.
    • An Auro master always uses Auro's renderer, whatever this is set to. It is already binaural by the time it is decoded, and rendering it again would spatialise it twice.

    • SOFA (HRTF) is the alternative: our own convolution, using an HRTF file you supply. It works for any layout with directions, including ones Auro does not carry — and if the Source layout is one Auro cannot render, the Player falls back to it and tells you so in Settings.

    Settings

    • Binaural renderer — Auro or SOFA (HRTF).
    • The HRTF used by the SOFA renderer. One ships with the Player; load your own .sofa if you have one. Not used while Auro is the renderer, and shown as unavailable then rather than looking live.

    • Check for Updates, and whether to check on launch.

    Known limitations

    • It cannot share an audio interface with soundBlade. Both open the device; whichever gets there first keeps it. Run one at a time, or give them different devices.
    • Its settings are its own, separate from soundBlade's, with the deliberate exception of the HRTF choice — that describes your ears and your room, so both applications follow it.

    • Nothing here writes to your files. The Player reads. Edit Metadata is the one exception and it asks first.

    For testers

    The things most worth breaking:

    • Binaural on a layout Auro does not carry — it should fall back to SOFA and say so in Settings, not go quiet or render something it should not.

    • Binaural loudness against the speaker render. Toggling it should not jump in level, and should not clip.

    • Open a master whose layout your device cannot carry — a 7.1.4 file on an 8-output interface. It should fold to something the device can play and say what it is doing, not play to outputs that are not there.
    • Mute and unmute channels while it plays, in any order.
    • Re-patch outputs with the Out column, then check the file still plays to where you put it after reopening.

    • Compare an Auro carrier against the same material decoded elsewhere.
